What you are building

Two 1 L containers joined by a salt-water-soaked cotton rope. Bacteria in the sludge container give electrons to the aluminum mesh, the electrons travel through your wires and meter to the second mesh, and ions cross through the rope to balance the charge. Expect a few hundred millivolts at best (the Instructable targets 300 mV), enough to read on a multimeter but not enough to charge a phone.

The materials list comes from the Instructable. Steps, quantities and the salt recipe are a general two-chamber recipe I wrote to fit those materials, because I could not retrieve the original page's steps. If the Instructable's blueprint gives different dimensions, follow it.

Troubleshooting

  • Reads zero: check every clip and that the rope touches liquid at both ends.
  • Reads negative: swap the probes.
  • Jumps high, then fades in hours: this is probably corrosion of the aluminum, not bacteria. Real bacterial power builds slowly over days.
  • Very low voltage: use a bigger mesh, a shorter rope, more salt in the rope water, or a warmer spot.
  • Readings drift when you touch things: keep wires still and the clips out of the water.

Safety

  • Wear gloves with sludge and wash your hands afterward. Keep it away from kids, pets and food.
  • Do not seal the sludge container completely airtight. Gas can build up, so leave a small gap or loosen the lid.
  • Return the sludge to where you found it when you finish. Do not pour it down an indoor drain.
  • This is about 0.3 V. Never connect it to mains power or a wall charger.
